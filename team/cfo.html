Chief Financial Officer

Daniel Osei

Owns the balance sheet — and the uncomfortable questions about it.

Daniel is responsible for the family's financial picture as a single object: every entity, custodian, mandate and obligation consolidated into one quarterly view that the family can actually act on. Where that view is incomplete, closing the gap is his job before anything else is discussed.

He came to Kroonenburg from a multi-family office, where he ran consolidated reporting for several balance sheets at once, and before that from audit and corporate finance. The combination shows in how he works: he will not present a number he cannot trace to source.

What he handles

Treasury and liquidity planning, cash and FX management across currencies, consolidated performance and exposure reporting, and the annual budget for both the office and the household. He selects, monitors and — when the mandate stops earning its fee — replaces external managers.

He coordinates the family's tax position with external advisers in each jurisdiction, owns the audit relationship, and prepares the material behind every capital decision that comes to the table.

How he frames decisions

Daniel presents capital proposals with the downside first: what has to be true for this to work, what it costs if it does not, and what the same money does if left where it is. Cost, liquidity and tax drag are treated as first-order terms, not footnotes.
